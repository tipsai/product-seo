## What this is for

Other sites mentioning the product. Search engines read those mentions as a sign the product exists and matters. AI assistants read catalogues and "best X" lists when someone asks them to recommend a tool, so being in those lists is also how a product shows up in their answers.

## Who does what

The agent finds the places, drafts every text for every form, and keeps the checklist. A human fills in the forms, confirms emails and sends messages. Do not submit forms or send outreach yourself.

## Step 1: where the product already is

- The links report in Search Console.
- A search for the brand name.
- The owner's memory.

Write the result into `seo/listings/already-listed.md`. Do not create a second profile where one exists; open the existing one and list what is outdated there.

## Step 2: the listing kit

Copy `assets/listing-kit.md` to `seo/listings/kit.md` and fill it from `seo/product-facts.md`. Forms ask for the same things in different lengths, so prepare all of them once:

- tagline up to 60 characters; short description up to 160; medium up to 300; long up to 700;
- categories and tags;
- pricing type and starting price, if the owner allows a price;
- platforms;
- captions for five product images;
- site, support address, social profiles, legal name and address if forms ask for them;
- a short email to authors of roundups.

Rule for the person submitting: copy as is, add nothing. If a form asks for something the kit lacks, ask the owner.

## Step 3: the list of places

`assets/directories.csv` is a ready list of places for an English-language AI software product, with the date each address was last checked. Copy it to `seo/listings/checklist.csv` and add status and resulting URL columns.

The goal of a listing is presence: the product named and linked on many relevant sites, so that Google and AI assistants can see that it exists and what it does. Visits and signups from a directory are a bonus, not the measure of success. A listing that sends no visitors has still done its job.

Tell the owner plainly what to expect:

- Many catalogues mark their links nofollow; check each finished listing. One listing will not move rankings. The value is cumulative: the same name, description and link in many places.
- The listing must be real: a true description on a catalogue people use. Google's guidance for AI search tells site owners they can ignore "pursuing inauthentic mentions". Presence built from honest listings is the opposite of that.
- A real article or roundup that mentions the product weighs more than a catalogue entry. Do both, and give "Getting into other people's lists" below its share of the time.

The `verified` column records what we happened to see from a place (`signups`, `visits`, `not measured`). It is an observation, not a rating of whether the place is worth doing. `link_status` says whether the address answered on `checked_on`.

Suggested order: AI tool directories first, because assistants read them; then software catalogues and "alternative to" listings that match the comparison pages; then launch platforms; then company profiles and second-line catalogues.

The list is for one kind of product in one market. For another niche or language, build your own with the same method: search for "submit your tool" and "add your product" pages in the niche, keep the ones that are indexed and that real people visit.

Keep the checklist current: the status of each submission and the address of the finished listing, so the listings can be found and updated when the product changes. If analytics show visits or signups from a place, note that too.

Working rules:

- Addresses of submission forms change. Open each one on the day it is used.
- A realistic pace is 15–30 minutes per place and five to eight places a day.
- Nobody pays for a listing without the owner's decision.
- A rejection is marked with its reason.

## Publishing under your own name

Platforms where the team posts its own articles: industry media with open publishing, blogging platforms, professional networks. There is no application to approve here. The article has to be worth reading.

- Write for that platform's readers: a story with a point, your product inside it where it belongs. A blog article pasted unchanged rarely works.
- If the platform allows it, set a canonical link to the original when republishing, or the copy competes with it.
- Which platform works depends on the market. One example from our own work: for a Russian-language product, our articles on vc.ru, published under our own name, brought signups and were among its strongest sources outside search. That is one product in one market. Find the platform your customers read and test it with two or three articles.

## Mention exchange with neighbour products

Products with a similar audience and no direct competition agree to mention each other in their roundups.

How to choose partners: same market, a blog that real people read, a product you would not be embarrassed to stand next to. Decline products whose business makes a bad neighbourhood and blogs that are empty. Keep it to a few partners.

Your side: a new, useful roundup on a topic where the partner fits, in preference to inserting mentions into old pages.

Be honest with the owner about the risk. Google's spam policies name as link spam: "Excessive link exchanges ('Link to me and I'll link to you')", along with partner pages made only for cross-linking. No number is given. Our reading: a few mutual mentions that are relevant and useful to the reader are not what the policy describes; a systematic scheme run for rankings is. Show the owner the quote and let them decide.

## Getting into other people's lists

1. For each pile A query, find the roundups that rank on the first page.
2. For each, find the author or the contact address.
3. Draft a short factual message from the kit: what the product does, who it is for, one thing it does that the listed ones do not, a link. No flattery and no pressure.
4. If a list already mentions the product with a wrong fact, send the correction. That is the easiest message to get answered.
5. The owner sends the messages.

## Common mistakes

- Small errors spread. A wrong domain ending or an outdated number in one old text gets copied into every new listing. The kit states the single correct value for each.
- Not recording submissions. Months later nobody can tell a listing that was never sent from one that was rejected. Fill in the status column on the day of submission.
- Skipping AI tool directories because they send little traffic. They are among the lists assistants read first.
- Judging a listing by the visits it sends. Its job is presence.
